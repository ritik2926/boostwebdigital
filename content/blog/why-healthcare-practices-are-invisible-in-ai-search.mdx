A dermatology practice we looked at recently holds the #1 organic spot on Google for its city's highest-volume search term. Ask ChatGPT, Perplexity, or Google's own AI Overview the same question a patient would actually type, and that practice never comes up. Three different competitors do — none of them ranked above position four.

That gap is not a fluke, and it is not a temporary bug in how AI systems work. It is the predictable result of optimizing for one system while a second, differently-wired system quietly became the one patients actually read.

## Why "ranking" and "being named" are different games

Search engine rankings and AI answers look similar from the outside — both are a response to a typed question — but they are produced by different processes, scored against different criteria, and won by different kinds of evidence.

### The blue link vs. the answer

A ranking is a position in a list. The user still has to click, read, and decide. An AI answer is a decision already partially made — the system has already read several sources, synthesized them, and handed the patient a short list of names it judged trustworthy enough to say out loud. Being third in that list beats being first in the one nobody reads anymore.

### What AI systems actually read

Traditional SEO rewards a well-structured page, solid backlinks, and keyword relevance on your own domain. AI systems weight something else more heavily: whether your practice's identity — name, location, specialty, credentials — reads identically everywhere it appears, and whether independent third parties (review sites, directories, press, other patients) describe you consistently. Your website is one input among many, not the whole picture.

That's not a niche behavior. It's the default. A practice's entire digital marketing plan can be built around a results page that most searching patients no longer scroll down to.

## Why your rankings don't automatically transfer

The instinct is to assume good SEO is good GEO by another name. Some of the underlying work overlaps — site speed, clear content, real expertise — but the scoring is different enough that a page can succeed at one and fail at the other.

### Entity consistency beats keyword density

AI systems build an internal model of "who you are" from every mention of your practice across the web. If your Google Business Profile lists one name, your website another, and a directory a third slightly different variant, the system doesn't average them — it hesitates, and hesitant entities get left out of a confident answer. Keyword-optimized copy on your own site does nothing to fix a mismatch that lives somewhere else entirely.

### Third-party validation carries more weight

Your own marketing copy describing your practice as trusted, experienced, or leading carries close to zero weight — anyone can write that about themselves. What AI systems actually cite is what other sources say about you: review content, directory listings, local press, and patient-generated mentions. This is the same shift that happened to SEO with backlinks a decade ago, compressed into a much shorter window and applied to language instead of links.

Even a practice that does get surfaced by an AI system still has to clear a second filter before it turns into a booking.

## Where patients actually start the search now

The shift isn't only about how the search engine responds — it's about which tool the patient opens first.

<Stat value="36%" label="of patients now use an AI tool to find a provider, ahead of Google search at 34% and physician referrals at 32%." />

### The AI-first patient journey

For more than a third of prospective patients, your website's search ranking is now a secondary signal at best. The AI conversation is the first touchpoint, and if your practice doesn't come up in it, your ranking never gets the chance to matter. This is the practical reason GEO isn't an optional add-on to a marketing plan built around traditional SEO — for over a third of the audience, it's the primary channel.

## Closing the visibility gap

None of this requires abandoning SEO. It requires treating entity consistency and third-party proof as their own workstream, not an afterthought bolted onto a keyword strategy.

### Fix your entity data everywhere it appears

Audit every place your practice's name, address, phone number, and specialty appear online, and make them identical, character for character. This includes your Google Business Profile, your website's footer and schema markup, every directory listing, and your social profiles. Inconsistency anywhere in that list is a real cost, not a cosmetic issue.

### Build the third-party signals AI actually cites

A steady flow of recent, detailed reviews, accurate directory listings, and mentions from sources you don't control are what AI systems treat as evidence. This is slower to build than a website redesign, and it compounds — every consistent, recent mention makes the next one easier for an AI system to trust.
